# Testing Borno with 3–5 children

Goal: find out what confuses children, what they enjoy and whether they
learn – before adding more features. Five children find most problems;
even three help a lot.

This guide needs **no tools**: a phone or tablet with the game, this page
printed (or on paper), and a pen.

---

## 1. Before the sessions

**Children**
- 3–5 children aged 3–6. Try to mix ages (one 3-year-old, one 6-year-old)
  and include at least one child who does not know any letters yet.

**Permission (always)**
- Ask a parent for permission in person and explain: "We are testing a
  learning game, not your child. It takes about 15 minutes. You can stay,
  and you can stop at any time."
- The parent stays in the room (or at least nearby).
- **Do not** take photos, videos or voice recordings of the children.
  **Do not** write down full names – use codes: C1, C2, C3 …
  Write only the age (e.g. "C2, 4 years").
- If a child does not want to play or gets upset: stop. That is a result too.

**Device**
- Charge it, turn the volume up, turn notifications off.
- Open the game. If you have recorded voices (Parents → Recording session),
  the children will hear them – that helps a lot.
- **Before each child:** Parents → **Reset** (so every child starts fresh).

## 2. During a session (10–15 minutes)

Say as little as possible. Your job is to **watch**, not to teach.
Only help if the child is stuck for more than about 20 seconds – and
write down that you helped.

| # | Task | What you say |
|---|------|-------------|
| 1 | First look | Give the device on the home screen. "This is a game. Show me what you can do." Wait 30 s. |
| 2 | Tracing | If they have not found it: "Can you press the pencil?" Let them trace 3–4 letters. |
| 3 | Watch | On a letter they find hard: "Can you find a button that shows you how to write it?" |
| 4 | A game | "Press খেলা and choose a game." Let them play 1–2 games. |
| 5 | Words | If age 5–6: open **শব্দ বানাও** and let them try 2 words. |
| 6 | End | "Which part did you like best? Which part was hard?" |

## 4. After the session – ask the parent (2 minutes)
1. Would your child want to play this again? Why / why not?
2. Was anything worrying or unsuitable for your child?
3. Did you understand the parent page (অভিভাবক)?
4. How often would your child play (every day / weekly / rarely)?

## 5. Optional: does the child learn? (after 1 week)
If a family keeps the game for a week:
- Day 1: show 6 letter cards on paper (অ আ ই ক খ গ) and note which ones the
  child can name. Do **not** teach.
- Day 7: show the same 6 cards again. Note which ones the child names now.
- Parents → progress shows play time per day and medals per letter – copy
  the numbers (no names).

## 6. Putting the results together
After all children, make one short list:

| Problem | How many children (of 5) | Screen | Idea to fix |
|---------|--------------------------|--------|-------------|
| e.g. did not understand ✔ | 3 | Tracing | bigger ✔, voice says "press ✔" |

Fix the problems seen with **2 or more children first**. Problems with only
one child: note them and watch for them next time.
